Your task is to identify conceptual synonyms that can be safely merged for descriptor normalization in co-word analysis. A conceptual synonym is a different expression that refers to the identical underlying concept.

To maintain maximum academic rigour while preserving effective bibliometric consolidation, propose a merge only when two terms refer to the exact same concept under one of the following criteria:

1. **Direct textual or morphological equivalence**
   - Word-order variations.
   - Singular/plural forms.
   - Grammatical inflections.
   - Orthographic or punctuation variants.
   These differences must not change the underlying referent.

2. **Organizational or token equivalence**
   - A term and a variant containing an organizational or entity-designating token (e.g., *project*, *program*, *initiative*, *undertaking*) may be merged only when the domain literature establishes that both expressions are alternative names for the exact same referent.
   - Do **not** merge a general concept, method, or research area with a specific project, program, organization, or initiative merely because they share the same lexical core.

3. **Established definitional equivalence**
   - The terms are recognized in the domain literature as strictly synonymous, with no conceptual difference.

### Exclusion Rules

- Do **not** merge terms based only on thematic similarity, topical overlap, relatedness, plausibility, or inferred functional similarity.
- Do **not** merge a base concept with a qualifier that denotes a distinct substantive attribute, subtype, methodology, application, or scientific concept.
- Do **not** merge terms from different clusters. Consider only terms within the same cluster.
- When there is any reasonable doubt that the two terms denote the identical concept, exclude the pair.

### Constraints

- Every proposed pair must contain terms that literally occur in the dataset.
- The only exception is singular/plural generation for the **variant** (the second tuple element). If an accepted variant has a regular singular or plural counterpart, generate both mappings even if only one form appears in the dataset. Never generate or modify the preferred term.
- Choose the preferred term as the descriptor most appropriate for co-word analysis. If both are equally appropriate, prefer the shorter term.
- Sort the final list alphabetically by preferred term and then by variant term.

### Output

Return **only** a Python list of tuples in the following format:

```python
[
    ("preferred_term_1", "variant_term_1"),
    ("preferred_term_2", "variant_term_2"),
]
```
